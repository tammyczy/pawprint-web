Overview
Create a 1–2 minute short film or video using AI as a meaningful part of the production process.
Your project may combine text-to-video, image-to-video, AI-generated imagery, AI animation, AI
voice/audio, AI-assisted editing, traditional filmmaking, or other creative techniques.
The goal is not simply to generate impressive clips. Strong projects should demonstrate
storytelling, visual direction, consistency, intentional use of AI, and thoughtful human editing
and decision-making.
This track is open to all grades and encourages creativity, technical skill, and thoughtful application
of AI.

The Future We Choose – Create a short video showing how AI impacts daily life, local culture, or
social challenges.

💡 Suggested Classroom Activity: Direct the Same Scene
Give students the same starting idea, such as:
“A robot discovers a flower in an abandoned city.”
Ask students to create different versions of the scene by changing one element at a time, such
as camera movement, framing, lighting, visual style, or character motion.
Have students compare the results and discuss:
How did each prompt change the final video?
Which instructions did the AI understand well?
Which instructions were difficult for the AI to follow?
How can prompt wording influence storytelling and visual style?
What responsibilities should creators consider when using AI-generated video?
This activity helps students practice prompt iteration, creative storytelling, filmmaking
concepts, and critical thinking about generative AI.

LLM & AI AGENT TRACK
Theme: AI as a Service
Your task is to instruct the AI to adopt a specific domain of expertise. This is not simply about
asking an AI a question or giving it a role. Your goal is to turn a general-purpose Large Language
Model into a knowledgeable and personalized advisor that can understand different users and
provide recommendations based on their individual needs.
Users may have different ages, interests, goals, experience levels, budgets, preferences, limitations,
or circumstances. Your advisor should be able to recognize these differences and adapt its
responses accordingly.
For example, a basketball shoe advisor might recommend different shoes to a beginner, an
experienced player, or someone recovering from an injury. A gardening advisor might give different
recommendations to someone living in an apartment versus someone with a large backyard.
The goal is to create an advisor that is not only knowledgeable, but also useful, reliable, adaptable,
and user-centered.
This track is designed for upper-middle and high school students who have a strong interest in
exploring Prompt Engineering in a research-oriented way. Students from all levels are welcome.
However, there are no separate categories for different age groups in this track.

LLM PROMPT ENGINEERING TRACK
Examples: 
1. Basketball Shoe Sales Assistant (Main Example)
Description of Idea: 
Design an AI to act as a sales assistant in a store specializing in basketball shoes. It should
understand the nuances of selecting the right basketball shoe based on various factors like playing
style, court type, and foot shape.
Prompt Construction: 
Research Phase: Begin by searching reputable sources and grasping essential details related to
basketball shoes. Understand the differences brought about by playing style, court type, foot
shape, and other unique factors. Includes any online resources you are using for constructing. For
this example, we are taking ideas about how to choose the right basketball shoes from this blog to
help generate the context and background. 
How To Choose The Right Basketball Shoes | Finish Line. (2023, June 13). The Fresh Press by Finish
Line. https://blog.finishline.com/how-to-choose-the-right-basketball-shoes/.
Initial Context & Background Setting: Using the gathered information, construct an initial context
for the AI. This should include foundational advice on shoe selection, emphasizing factors exclusive
to basketball shoes.
Test & Refine: Pretend to be a potential customer and engage with the AI. Identify areas where the
assistant might falter in its recommendations or knowledge. Adjust the context and background
setting based on these interactions, refining the prompts to enhance its proficiency.
Finalization: Once satisfied with the AI's performance in its specialized domain, finalize the context
and background settings for optimal user interactions.

Tips:
3. Design for different people
Ask yourself: Would my advisor give the same recommendation to everyone? If yes, it probably
needs more personalization. Think about age, experience, goals, interests, budget, preferences,
and other relevant constraints.
4. Ask before advising
A good advisor may need additional information before making a recommendation. Teach your
AI to ask useful follow-up questions rather than immediately guessing what the user needs.
5. Try to make your AI fail
Testing is not about proving that your advisor is perfect. Ask difficult questions and look for
mistakes, weak recommendations, missing information, or made-up facts. Then improve it.
6. Know when to say “I don't know.”
A reliable advisor should recognize uncertainty. Sometimes asking the user for more information
or recommending that they verify something is better than confidently giving an incorrect
answer.
